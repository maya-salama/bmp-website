Finding a Therapist Who Gets It

By Board Member•September 2026•6 min read
[ Cover image ]

One of the most common things we hear is: "I want therapy, but I don't want to explain my entire religion to my therapist before we even get to the actual problem." That exhaustion is real, and it's one of the biggest barriers keeping Muslim students from seeking support. Here's how to shortcut that search.

Start with campus resources — but ask specific questions

Our campus counseling center is free and confidential, and it's worth starting there even if you're not sure it's the right long-term fit. When you book your first session, you can ask directly whether any counselors have experience working with Muslim or religious clients. You are allowed to request a different counselor if the fit isn't right — that's normal, not rude.

Questions worth asking a potential therapist

  • Have you worked with Muslim clients before, or had training in religious/cultural competence?
  • How do you usually handle topics like prayer, modesty, family expectations, or fasting if they come up?
  • Are you open to me bringing in my faith as a resource, not just a source of stress?

Where else to look

Beyond campus counseling, there are directories specifically for Muslim therapists and culturally competent providers — we keep an updated list on our Resources page. If cost is a barrier, ask any provider directly about sliding-scale rates; many offer them but don't advertise it.

You deserve care that doesn't ask you to leave part of yourself at the door.
