What Our First Support Circle Taught Us

By Board Member•September 2026•4 min read
[ Cover image ]

We weren't sure anyone would show up to our first peer support circle. It felt like a risk — asking students to sit in a room and talk about things we're usually taught to keep private. Eight people came. By the end, most of us were in tears, and not the kind that need fixing.

Community care isn't a replacement for therapy

We want to be clear about this: a support circle is not therapy, and it's not meant to replace a licensed counselor. What it does offer is something clinical care sometimes can't — the specific relief of being in a room with people who don't need the cultural or religious context explained to them.

What we learned for next time

  • Confidentiality has to be stated out loud, every single time, before anyone shares.
  • Having a loose structure (a prompt, a time limit per person) helped more than an open-ended "talk about anything" format.
  • Closing with a shared dua grounded the whole session in something bigger than the conversation itself.

If you want in on the next circle, keep an eye on our events page or join our mailing list so you hear first.
